# Bob-side concentration gate

Status: **built** (2026-11-08) in `swap-cli`: the reader (`swap-cli/src/watch/producers.rs`), the engine screen (`swap-cli/src/producer_gate.rs`, called from `bob_action` at `funding_final` in `swap-cli/src/engine.rs`), the per-profile numbers (`Gates` in `swap-cli/src/params.rs`), the flag `--accept-producer-concentration`, the helper setting `ACCEPT_PRODUCER_CONCENTRATION` and an alert for a removed claim. Tests: `swap-cli/tests/producer_gate.rs` and the unit tests in the same files. Nothing in it touches a chain beyond read-only header calls. The threat, the measurement and the model are in `docs/miner-concentration.md` (threat T21 in `docs/protocol-design.md` section 8); this document does not repeat them. Section 9 records the decisions that shaped the build; section 10 is about Bob's re-send after a reorg (an alert is built, an automatic re-send is not).

## Bottom line

- **The gate is a screen, not a defence.** Before Bob locks his ETH, `swap-cli` reads who produced the last 2,880 and the last 720 blocks of Parano1d from the node's own headers. Under the `production` profile it does not offer `lock_eth` when one address produced more than 35 % of either window, or when it cannot read the headers in full, they are stale, or the second Parano1d node disagrees. Under `sepolia` and `rehearsal` (limit 28 %) the same evaluation runs and only prints a warning.
- **Two largest together above 50 % is a warning in every profile**, never a refusal.
- **It stops a swap against a producer that is visibly large.** It does not stop one that hides behind fresh addresses, a pool, a partner, rented hashrate, or a share that grows after the lock. Section 2 lists what stays open.
- **Under `production` the producer check needs the second Parano1d node**: `--single-node-ok` waives the F5 rule but not this; only `--accept-producer-concentration` does.
- **As of the measurement it would refuse every production swap**, and it would have refused every swap for the last 20 days.
- **An explicit flag, `--accept-producer-concentration`, waives the production refusals.** It is printed on every tick and recorded once in the swap file's history, and no profile implies it.
- **A removed claim now raises an alert** (a note that starts with `ALERT:`, and an optional message from the watch loop). **No automatic re-send is built** (section 10).

## 1. Purpose and threat

1. If one party holds more than half of the hashrate of Parano1d and is Alice, she can reveal the secret on Ethereum, claim Bob's ETH, then keep Bob's NOID claim from becoming final until `D` and take the NOID back. Bob loses.
2. Above half of the hashrate no window length helps; below it the window and Bob's re-send speed decide (`docs/miner-concentration.md`, sections 3 to 4).
3. Bob can see, before he locks, who produced the recent blocks: every Parano1d header carries the producer's address.
4. The gate refuses to lock (production) or warns (the other profiles) when the recent producers are too concentrated. It looks backwards, at one address at a time (and the top two together, as a warning).
5. It concerns Bob only. Alice's side has no such exposure (`docs/miner-concentration.md`, section 4.1), and Alice's role never reads headers.
6. It fails closed under production: if the headers cannot be read in full, are stale, or the two nodes disagree, there is no lock.

## 2. What the gate can and cannot do

**It can** refuse a swap (production) or warn about one when the node's own headers show a producer above a stated share of the recent blocks. It costs Bob nothing when the chain is not concentrated, and a swap that is refused loses nothing: Bob has locked nothing.

**It cannot defend.** What stays open, each of these defeats or weakens the screen:

| Open | Why |
|---|---|
| One miner on many addresses | The header names the address that gets the reward. An operator can split over fresh addresses; a measured share is a lower bound for that operator. |
| A pool | A pool pays one address. The gate sees the pool as one producer; whoever runs the pool can attack, and the gate cannot tell. |
| Several large producers acting together | The top-two rule is a crude cover, and only a warning. Three or more parties together are not covered. Today 10 distinct addresses produce blocks; the largest three together held 85.9 % of the last 24 hours. |
| Rented or switched hashrate | The gate looks backwards at 24 hours and 6 hours. A miner who joins after the lock, or rents hashrate for the swap, is not in the window. Bob's exposure runs from his lock to `D` (about 6 hours at 30 s per block). |
| A past share is not the future share | In the sustained period the largest share changed by 3 points or less from one day to the next in 11 of the 20 steps and by 3.2 to 4.2 points in 6, but by 9.5 points in one step, 7.8 in another and 5.0 in a third; on sliding windows the same address's share in the next 738 blocks exceeded its previous 2,880-block share by 6.2 points at the 90th percentile and 16.6 at the 99th, and 24 of the 231 starts that the 35 % gate would have passed were followed by a stretch above 42 % (one episode, the entry of the dominant address; `docs/miner-concentration.md`, section 5). The 5-point allowance below is a judgement, not a bound: the data shows larger moves. The limit is not moved for it: in that one episode a lower limit removed bad passes only together with passing starts (35 %: 24 bad of 231 passing; 33 %: 21 of 210; 30 %: 16 of 87; 28 %: 3 of 18; 25 %: 0 of 1), so the share of bad passes stayed between 10 % and 18 % until almost nothing passed. One episode says nothing general about tighter limits. |
| The reward address is not the worker | The block template accepts any valid address for an external worker. A new attacker can mine to a fresh address and show no history. |
| No exit after the lock | Once Bob has locked, only Alice's reveal or the timeout ends the swap. The gate does not watch the share during the swap, and it is not applied to the re-send of a dropped lock (the exposure decision was taken at the first send). A `lock_eth` intent that is already recorded is not withdrawn if the screen fails later: the recorded intent is what `live.js` sends, as with every other gate. Since 2026-11-10 the tick says so when that happens (an `ALERT:` note that the pending lock must not be sent and how to cancel the intent, section 4), and the tool still does not cancel the intent itself. |
| The headers are trusted | `swap-cli` does not check proof of work in a header, and does not recompute a header's hash. A node that lies is caught only by the second node: the hash and the producer of the block at the anchor (three below the lower tip) and of blocks drawn afresh on every tick from the short window (32 heights) and from the long window (32 heights) are compared (section 4, "Two nodes"), and one detection is remembered for the rest of the swap (section 4, "Remembered detection"). That makes a first node that reports true hashes beside false producers risk detection, but it is a sample, not a proof, and the chance of missing depends on how much it hides: to bring a producer of share `p` under the limit `L` in one window, the node must relabel `p - L` of that window, and a draw of 32 heights from that window then misses every relabelled block with chance `(1 - (p - L))^32`. At the production limit of 35 % that is about 19 % for a producer of 40 %, 3.4 % for 45 % and 0.26 % for 52 %; a node that hides a producer only a few points over the limit is still missed often (a producer of 36 % hidden by 1 point is missed 72 % of the time). Section 4 gives the figures and says why they hold per swap. It does **not** help when the two nodes both lie, or share one lying upstream (two URLs are not two independent nodes, and the tool does not check that they are), nor when one party mines to several addresses (the first row). The header's hash is still not recomputed from its fields and the second node is never asked for a `prev_hash`, so a first node can give a relabelled block a made-up hash, with a matching false `prev_hash` on the block above it. That is caught, and remembered, when a sampled height lands on such a block while the second node names the anchor block again after the sample; it is not caught at a block that is not sampled, nor in the up to five blocks above the anchor, which the second node is never asked about. Recomputing each hash would close more of it and is not built. The screen is a screen, not a defence. |
| Refusing is not free | Under production it also refuses honest Alices. Availability falls to zero while one address holds more than the limit. |
| Other pairings | The thresholds are derived for the Ethereum pairing and its guaranteed spans (361 blocks production, 132 sepolia). A Bitcoin pairing has other spans (`D_offset` 2,050) and needs its own derivation. |
| Model gaps | Deeper reorgs than seen, timestamp choices on a private branch, and the repeated game with the difficulty rule are not modelled (`docs/miner-concentration.md`, sections 2 and 7). |

## 3. The data

**Source.** The node's own headers through `paranoid_getBlockHeader(height)`, field `miner` (the coinbase recipient, bech32m). Never a third-party service: that is the point of reading it from Bob's own node, and `swap-cli` already asks the same call for the tip's timestamp (F5). The reply also carries `hash`, `prev_hash` and `timestamp` (`noid_rpc/src/types.rs`, `BlockHeaderInfo`). A header's hash covers the miner address, and the hash of a header covers its parent's hash.

**Where it is read.** Only by `swap-cli tick`, only for Bob's role, only while the file is in `received`, `funded` or `funding_final` (the phases in which a lock can still be decided), and only against a live node (`--noid-rpc`; a fixture replay reads none). `claim`, `intent` and Alice's ticks read none. The reader uses its own transports, never the recording one, so none of its headers ends up in a recorded fixture. The watcher's own F5 call for the tip header (it reads the timestamp only) does go through the recording transport; the recorder now replaces the `miner` field of any recorded `paranoid_getBlockHeader` reply with a fixed label, so a recording holds no producer address (recordings made before this change may). The reader's per-call timeout is capped at 10 s and the whole read, the second node's headers included, shares one 40 s budget (a call already under way when it ends can overrun it by at most the per-call cap).

**JSON-RPC batching (measured, 2026-11-08, read-only, local node).** One batch of 50 `paranoid_getBlockHeader` calls was sent to the node on the local machine: accepted (HTTP 200), the answer was an array of 50 objects in request order with no error, 32,791 bytes, 150 ms including the start of the `curl` process. A batch of 250 was also accepted: an array of 250 in order, 164,141 bytes (about 0.65 KB per header), 281 ms. Heights were contiguous and every `prev_hash` was the `hash` of the header below it. So the node serves batches of 250, and a first read of 2,880 headers is 12 requests. The implementation does not rely on it: `JsonRpc::call_batch` has a default that sends the calls one at a time, the HTTP and `curl` transports send an array body and match the answers by `id`, and the reader asks again with single calls whenever the answer is not an array of the right length (a node that refuses batches answers with one error object), when the transport fails on the batch, or for any item of a batch that came back as an error.

**The cache.** A rolling file beside the swap file, `<swap file>.producers` (not the swap file itself, so a swap file made before the change loads unchanged), one line per height: `height hash parent_hash timestamp miner`, ascending and gap-free, written atomically and readable by its owner only. The producer is kept as the address string in this local file, because it has to be counted; it is never printed (a producer is shown as the first 8 hex characters of the SHA-256 of its address) and the file is not committed. It holds the long window (and up to 64 heights below it as the window slides), about 0.6 MB.

Each tick:
1. Take the tip `T` from the observation (`paranoid_blockCount`, already read). The long window is the heights `T - 2,879 ..= T`. A chain shorter than the window is incomplete.
2. Load the cache; a file that does not parse, has a gap, a line that does not link to the one above, or does not start at or below the window's first height is discarded. Drop cached heights above `T`.
3. Read the header at the cache's highest height. If its hash is the cached hash, the cache is good. If not, a reorg happened since the last tick: read downwards, eight heights at a time, at most 64 deep, to the highest height whose hash still matches; drop everything above it. If nothing matches within 64, discard the cache. The depth of the mismatch (65 means none matched) is kept in the summary as a log-only field (`mismatch_depth`); nothing reads it.
4. Read the missing heights up to `T`, oldest first, in batches of up to 250.
5. Check the link: for every height, `prev_hash` equals the hash of the header below it, across the batches and the junction with the cache. A break means a reorg during the read: that batch is thrown away, the summary is incomplete with the reason, and the next tick reads again.
6. Count the long window (2,880 heights) and the short window (720) ending at `T`: the largest producer, the two largest together, the number of distinct producers.

**When to start, and the budget.** As soon as the swap file is in `received` (Bob has Alice's bundle), so that the window is ready when funding is final (at least 19 blocks, about 10 minutes, after Alice's funding). A tick reads for at most 40 s of wall clock and goes on at the next tick; the summary says how many headers are read so far. In `received` and `funded` the reading changes no decision; only `lock_eth` waits for it. With a warm cache a tick reads one to three headers.

**What the observation holds.** `NoidObservation.producers` (absent when nothing was read; `skip_serializing_if`, so old recordings and fixtures load unchanged): `source` (`scheme://host:port`), `tip`, `header_tip`, `read_at`, the two window sizes, `have` (headers of the long window held), `long` and `short` (window size, largest, 8-hex id of the largest, top two, distinct), `complete`, `headers_read`, `second` (the cross-check), `mismatch_depth`, `error`. It holds no address; the tick prints it with the rest of the observation.

## 4. The rule

All comparisons use integer counts (a share is `count / N`): over the limit when `count * 1000 > limit_permille * N`, so the boundary is exact. A share exactly at the limit passes. Shares are printed with one decimal, rounded up, so a printed share is above a limit exactly when the integer test says so.

**Windows.** Long window `N = 2,880` blocks (about 24 hours: the same unit as the measurement); short window `N = 720` (about 6 hours: the span of a swap, `D_offset` 738 in production, which catches a producer that appeared a few hours ago). Both must pass. The numbers live in `Gates` (`producer_window_long`, `producer_window_short`, `producer_limit_permille`, `producer_top_two_permille`, `producer_gate_enforced`) and `Gates::validate` checks them (limits between 1 and 999 permille, long at least short and short above 0, enforced exactly under `production`). They are not command-line options.

**The limit `X`, and where it comes from.** `X = L - 2 sigma - drift`. `L` is the working limit from `docs/miner-concentration.md`, section 5: the largest share at which the model keeps the attack under 0.1 % at the guaranteed span, with free ties and a private lead of 10 blocks (production 361 blocks: `L = 0.42`; sepolia 132 blocks: `L = 0.35`). `2 sigma` is 1.9 points (`N = 2,880`, worst case `p = 0.5`; 3.7 points for `N = 720`). `drift` is 5 points, **a judgement, not a measurement and not a bound**: in the sustained period the largest share changed by 3 points or less from one day to the next in 11 of the 20 steps, by 3.2 to 4.2 points in 6, and by 5.0, 7.8 and 9.5 points in the other three; on sliding windows its share in the next 738 blocks exceeded its previous 2,880-block share by 6.2 points at the 90th percentile, 16.6 at the 99th and 27.8 at most (`docs/miner-concentration.md`, section 5). A share can move by more than the allowance, and has.

| profile | `L` | minus 2 sigma | minus drift | `X` (set) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| production | 42 % | 1.9 (1.86 at the worst case, 1.84 near 0.42) | 5 | **35 %** (350 permille) |
| sepolia, rehearsal | 35 % | 1.9 (1.86 at the worst case, 1.78 near 0.35) | 5 | **28 %** (280 permille) |

The short window uses the same `X`. Its own error is larger, so on its own it refuses somewhat more often near the limit. That is the intended direction for a screen that fails closed.

**What each finding does.**

| # | Condition | production | sepolia, rehearsal |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | The largest producer's share of the long or the short window is above `X`. | `lock_eth` not offered; `Wait` with an ALARM reason | warning note; the lock is offered |
| R3 | The headers cannot be read in full: no summary, a call failed, a reply did not decode, a height is missing, the chain of `prev_hash` is broken, the budget ran out before the window was complete, or the summary was built for other windows. | as R1 | as R1 |
| R4 | The data is stale: the headers end more than 2 blocks below the node's tip (the F5 limit `MAX_NOID_TIP_DIFF`), or the read finished more than 120 s before the decision (`MAX_OBSERVATION_AGE_S`), or its time is unknown. (The first half is a backstop: the read is aimed at the observation's own tip, so in practice only a poll that takes more than 120 s makes a read stale.) | as R1 | as R1 |
| R5 | A second Parano1d node is configured and the block at the anchor height or at a sampled height differs, or names a different producer for a block with the same hash, or the cross-check (the anchor and the sampled heights of both windows) could not be read in full or did not run; or, under `production` with `--single-node-ok`, no second node was given (`--single-node-ok` does not relax this). | as R1 | warning note; the lock is offered |
| R6 | An earlier tick saw the two nodes name different producers for a block with equal hashes (the second node asked once more and still naming another producer), or hold different blocks at a sampled height below an anchor they named again after the sample, and wrote the marker `<swap file>.producer-differs` (which still exists or cannot be read) and, with `--apply`, the history entry `producer_differs_seen` of the swap file (which is there). | as R1, for the rest of the swap | warning note on every tick; the lock is offered |
| R2 | The two largest together hold more than 50 % of either window. | warning note only | warning note only |

**Two nodes.** Under `production` and `sepolia`, F5 already requires a second Parano1d node for `lock_eth` (or `--single-node-ok`). The reader reuses it: the anchor height is `min(tip_1, tip_2) - 3`, and the second node is asked for the block there. Because every hash covers its parent's hash, **equal hashes at the anchor mean the two nodes hold the same chain below it**, producers included, provided each node reports the real hash of its header (the reader does not recompute a hash, see section 2). A node can report a true hash beside a false producer, so, once the anchor block agrees in hash and producer, the reader also draws two samples, afresh on every tick from the operating system's random generator (the source the secrets come from; the count `SECOND_SAMPLE_COUNT = 32` is a constant in the code, not a command-line option): **32 distinct heights from the short window's range** (the first height of the last 720 blocks up to the anchor) and **32 distinct heights from the long window's range** (the first height of the last 2,880 up to the anchor). Both windows are sampled because R1 refuses on either: a producer that is over the limit only in the short window (the rising producer the short window exists to catch) would otherwise be hidden by relabelling about 122 of the last 720 blocks, which is about 4 % of the long range, and a long-window draw alone would miss that about 26 % of the time. Heights are never above the anchor (where the two tips may differ) and never below the window; a range of fewer than 32 heights is sampled completely; the anchor itself can be drawn (the samples come from ranges that include it, so the number of other blocks is not exactly 32 per window). The union of the two draws (a height drawn in both is asked once) goes to the second node in one batch under the reading's own deadline (the total time budget is unchanged), and the **hash and the producer** of each height are compared with the first node's entry. `Agrees` carries `sampled`, the number of distinct heights compared besides the anchor: 64 when the two draws do not overlap, fewer when they do or a range is short. A different hash at the anchor is `Differs`; the same hash with another producer, at the anchor or at a sampled height, is `ProducerDiffers` once the second node, **asked once more for that one height**, gives the same hash and again a producer other than the first node's (a second node that takes its answer back, so that it names two different producers for one block, or answers with another hash, fails, or does not answer, makes the result `Unreadable` and nothing is remembered); a different hash at a sampled height below the anchor is `SampledDiffers` when the second node names the same anchor block again after the sample (and an ordinary `Differs` at the anchor, or `Unreadable`, when it does not). All of them are R5 and are refused alike in every profile that enforces the screen, `ProducerDiffers` and `SampledDiffers` are remembered (next paragraphs), and the summary carries only the height, never an address. A second node that does not return every sampled height in time, or answers with an error, makes the check unreadable (R5, fail closed under `production`).

**How likely a lying first node is to be missed, and why per swap.** A first node that wants to bring a producer of true share `p` down to the limit `L` in a window has to relabel `p - L` of that window's blocks. Each height of a draw of 32 from that window is then a relabelled one with chance `p - L`, and the draw misses all of them with chance `(1 - (p - L))^32`. **That per-window figure is the bound this text relies on.** A producer over the limit in only ONE window (the short one: a rising producer; the long one: a producer that has since faded) lets the liar place its relabels inside that window's own range, and the tick then misses with about the single-window chance: the short-window-only case is a little lower than the bound because the long draw also covers a few of its relabels (a 40 % producer: about 13 % against the bound of 19 %), the long-window-only case is the bound itself (the short draw lands where there are no relabels). Only when the producer has the SAME share in both windows must the liar relabel in both, and then the two draws are independent and the tick misses with about `(1 - (p - L))^64`. In percent, by true share `p`:

| true share `p` | production `L` = 35 %: one window (the bound) | production: the same share in both windows | sepolia and rehearsal `L` = 28 %: one window (the bound) | sepolia: the same share in both windows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | - | - | 52 | 27 |
| 0.35 | - | - | 9.8 | 0.96 |
| 0.36 | 72 | 53 | 6.9 | 0.5 |
| 0.40 | 19 | 3.7 | 1.7 | 0.03 |
| 0.45 | 3.4 | 0.12 | 0.26 | 0.0007 |
| 0.52 | 0.26 | 0.0007 | - | - |

(Caption: the first and third value columns are `(1 - (p - L))^32`, the second and fourth are `(1 - (p - L))^64`; a dash means the share is not above that limit, or the case is far below any figure shown. These are the formulas' values, not measurements: the code tests only upper bounds on the miss rate, section 7. They ignore the unsampled blocks above the anchor, below.) **Per swap.** The first detection is remembered (next paragraph), so a node that lies on every tick is stopped by the first tick that catches it. A lying first node can instead time its lie to ONE tick: it serves an honest window until the tick that decides the lock, and then reports a top header whose hash matches nothing within the cache's repair depth, which makes the reader drop its cache and read the whole window again in that very tick. The chance that the lock is offered against the lie is then the miss chance of that one tick, which is the per-window bound above (or the equal-share figure where the producer is over in both windows), for both kinds of detection: it does not shrink with the number of ticks, and it is not larger either, because the later steps that decide whether a sample's difference is remembered do not depend on the budget the first node can spend (timing notes below). Without the memory it would be worse: a tick that caught the lie would not stop the next tick, drawn afresh, from missing it. **Unsampled blocks above the anchor.** The anchor is three blocks below the lower of the two tips, and the first node's tip may be two above the second's, so up to FIVE blocks at the top of the first node's window are never compared with the second node's, and the formulas ignore them. A node that relabels there is not caught: for example, bringing a 36 % producer down to 35 % in the short window takes 8 relabelled blocks (36 % of 720 is 259.2 against a limit of 252), and 5 of those can sit above the anchor, leaving 3 in the range that is drawn from, which a draw of 32 misses with chance `(1 - 3/717)^32`, about 87 %. **Plainly:** a node that hides a producer only a few points over the limit is still missed often; the check makes a large lie expensive, not a small one impossible, and the screen stays what it says it is, a screen and not a defence. **What it does not cover:** one party on several addresses (the share each address shows is a lower bound); two lying nodes, or two URLs that share one lying upstream (the tool does not check independence); a wrong SECOND node, or a proxy or tunnel in front of it, can also trigger a detection, which is a refusal and never a wrongly allowed lock; hashes that are false as well as producers are caught only as described in the next paragraph (the hash is not recomputed from the header's fields); the unsampled blocks above the anchor. **Timing:** a second node that refuses batches gets the anchor call plus up to 64 single calls; the second node has only the reserve of the budget (5 s of the 40 s), so on a cold read a slow second node may be unreadable on that tick (fail closed) and fine on the next, when the first node's headers are cached and the whole reserve is its own. The anchor call and the sample batch share the reading's deadline, which the first node's own calls also spend: a first node can therefore make a tick end unread before or during the sample, which refuses that tick under `production` and is not remembered. It cannot choose that outcome AFTER seeing whether the sample hit, because it never sees the sample (the heights are drawn on this machine after the first node has answered). The further calls, the second ask of the anchor after a sample that showed a hash difference below the anchor and the confirmation of a producer difference (the second node asked for that one height again), have their own time, a fresh per-call timeout (10 s) each, and not the shared deadline; so only the second node's own failure or slowness can make them unreadable. They can extend a tick beyond its budget by at most one per-call timeout each (two, when the anchor's second ask shows another producer and is confirmed in turn), and only on a tick whose sample showed such a difference. The confirmation asks only the second node: the first node's entry is deliberately not read again, because a lying first node would answer that one call truthfully and be forgotten; the node that matters for the latch is the first one (its headers are what the screen counts), and an honest second node answers the same twice, so a lying first node is still remembered. A second node that takes back its own answer is refused for the tick and not remembered, which means a lying SECOND node can avoid the latch, but a lying second node beside an honest first node cannot hide anything (the counts come from the first node), and two lying nodes defeat the screen anyway.

**Remembered detection.** Two kinds of evidence cannot come from two honest nodes, and the tick that sees one writes a marker file beside the swap file, `<swap file>.producer-differs` (its own file, not part of the `.producers` cache, which can be deleted and rebuilt):

1. **Equal hashes with different producers** (`ProducerDiffers`, marker kind `producer`): equal hashes are the same block, so two honest nodes name the same producer for it. One answer is not enough to remember it for good: the second node is asked once more for that one height (its own fresh per-call timeout, the first node is not asked again), and the finding stands only if it gives the same hash and again a producer other than the first node's. A second node that names the first node's producer the second time (two producers for one block) gives an `Unreadable` result with that reason: the tick is refused under `production` and nothing is remembered. The same confirmation applies to a producer difference at the anchor and to one seen at the anchor's second ask (item 2).
2. **A different hash at a SAMPLED height below the anchor** (`SampledDiffers`, marker kind `hash`), when the second node names the same anchor block, in hash and producer, before the sample AND again after the sampled answers arrived. A first node can give a relabelled block a made-up hash, with a matching false `prev_hash` on the block above it (the reader recomputes no hash and never asks the second node for a `prev_hash`), so its own chain of headers still links and the anchor, which it leaves true, still agrees. Two honest nodes that agree on a block agree on every block below it, and an honest reorg would have moved the anchor, so a difference below an anchor that agreed before and still agrees after cannot be honest. The second ask of the anchor is made only when a sample shows such a difference. If the anchor changed between the two asks, or the second ask could not be read in time, the tick's result is an ordinary, unremembered `Differs` (at the anchor) or `Unreadable`.

**What is never remembered:** a different hash AT the anchor (an honest reorg near the tip can cause it), a missing height, a timeout or an error (`Unreadable`: fail closed for that tick). If one sample shows both a producer difference and a hash difference, the outcome is the remembered producer kind (an ordinary result never wins over a remembered one). The summary says which kind without holding an address: `producer_differs { height }` or `sampled_differs { height }`; a plain `differs` at the anchor is as before.

The marker is created exclusively (an existing marker is left exactly as it is: the first detection stays), written in full, synced, and its directory synced; it holds a version, the height and the time (Unix seconds), and for the hash kind `"kind":"hash"` (a record without `kind` is the producer kind): no address, no hash. It is written on a read-only `tick` as well as on `--apply`, for Bob's file only. **The tool never deletes or rewrites it, and a detection cannot be cleared for a swap file.** The marker is found by the swap file's path, so a memory that lives only there would be lost by deleting the file or by moving the swap file without it. The swap file's own history therefore holds the twin: with `--apply`, one history entry `producer_differs_seen` is written once per swap file when a detection is on record, that is, while a marker is present, or unreadable, or when this very tick made a detection (also when the marker could not be written at all). Once that entry is in the file, the screen refuses exactly as if the marker were there (the state `remembered`, with the time of the entry), whether or not the marker file exists and wherever the swap file is. Removing the marker, or moving or copying the swap file, reopens nothing. The two ways on are `--accept-producer-concentration` (printed as OVERRIDDEN on every tick and recorded in the history), or ending this swap (if no lock was sent there is nothing to refund) and starting a new one with a new swap file. A read-only tick writes no history entry (it can save nothing), so the memory is in the file only after a `tick --apply`; the helpers' loop runs one every tick. (The header cache `<swap file>.producers` keeps what a first node said earlier: a cache filled by a lying first node would be compared again with honest nodes. It belongs to the old swap file; a new swap file has its own. This is also why the entry is not re-read from the first node before it is remembered: a lying node would answer that one call truthfully and never be remembered; with an honest first node every entry of the window is tied to headers fetched in this tick, because the cache top is read again and its hash must equal the cached one, and the entries below it are linked to it by `prev_hash` as checked when they were read and when the cache file is parsed. What the cache cannot do is forget a lie told earlier. The effect is only a refusal, never an allowed lock.) Two honest ways to a false detection exist, both refusals only and ended the same way (a new swap file, or the override after finding the cause): the second node switching to another fork and back, deeper than the anchor distance, inside one batch round-trip (negligible); and a disk fault that changes a `miner` field of the cached headers, which gives a false producer-kind detection (a changed hash breaks the checked links and the cache is discarded, but a changed `miner` is not checked). A marker that is unreadable for a passing reason, such as a failed read, also becomes permanent for that swap file once an `--apply` tick has recorded it; that costs a new swap file, not funds, since nothing is locked yet. The detection is per swap file: a new swap file with the same nodes starts clean, so a node that was caught once should be treated as suspect by the operator. On every tick of Bob's file in the phases the screen applies to (`received`, `funded`, `funding_final`), the state is read: absent, present, unreadable, or remembered by the history alone. While it is not absent, **under `production` the screen refuses for the rest of the swap** with its own headline and a sentence that says what was seen (the kind), at which height and when, that the operator should compare the two nodes by hand, that the file is never removed by the tool, and that it cannot be cleared (the two ways on above); **under `sepolia` and `rehearsal` it warns on every tick and the lock is offered**, exactly as `Differs` does there. A marker that is unreadable or malformed counts as present under `production` (its own headline says the record cannot be read) and warns elsewhere. `--accept-producer-concentration` waives it exactly as it waives the rest of the screen: printed on every tick and recorded once in the history. A marker that could not be created (a read-only or missing folder) relaxes nothing: that tick refuses anyway, on the detection it carries in memory, and a note says the record was not created, with the error kind; on `--apply` the same tick writes the history entry (so the swap file itself remembers), and a read-only tick says plainly that nothing was saved and that `tick --apply` (or a fixed folder) is needed. A marker that was created but whose write or sync was not confirmed exists (an empty or partial one reads as unreadable, which counts as present), and the note says so. This is a new rule of Bob's lock gate, decision 2026-11-09; the confirmation of a producer difference, the history memory and the rule that a detection cannot be cleared are decision 2026-11-10, after an outside read (2026-11-10). No threshold, window, profile rule or override changed.

**A `lock_eth` intent that is already pending when the screen starts to refuse (decision 2026-11-10).** `tick --apply` records the `lock_eth` intent on a tick whose screen passes, and the helper that sends the lock sends the frozen parameters. A later tick can find a detection (or a share over the limit). `tick_apply` then holds the intent as before and adds one note that starts with `ALERT:` (printed on the first tick that sees the refusal, once per intent and kind of finding, because the alert hook of `scripts/live` sends one message per `ALERT:` line and expects one tick per alert; the latch is a log-only history entry `producer_alert_lock_pending:<decided_at>:<kind>` written by `--apply`, with the kind one of `concentration`, `cannot-be-checked`, `stale`, `disagree`, `one-node`, `remembered`; written again, with a new alert, when an override entry has come after the last latch of its kind (the flag was passed once and then dropped), so a transient finding (a second-node timeout) does not use up the alert of a later real detection): `ALERT: the producer screen refuses to lock now (T21: {headline}), but a lock_eth intent decided at {unix time} (Unix time) is still pending. Do NOT send that lock (do not run lock.sh or live.js lock). If nothing was sent, cancel the intent with `swap-cli intent FILE --cancel` (with the same chain flags as tick). If it may already have been sent, look at the contract`. The reason of the `wait` changes the same way, because the ledger's usual "reuse the printed parameters" would send the lock the screen refused. With `--accept-producer-concentration` the screen does not refuse and there is no alert. Under `sepolia` and `rehearsal` the screen only warns, so the same text comes on every tick as a `WARNING:` ("the producer screen holds a remembered detection ...") and only when the cause is a remembered detection. The tool does not cancel the intent by itself. The latch entry is the durable mark that this intent must not be sent, and it is written for every kind of refusal the screen makes (a share over the limit, data that cannot be verified or is stale, a second node missing or differing, a remembered detection), by `tick --apply` only (a read-only tick alerts and writes nothing). `live.js lock --swap FILE` reads, before any network call and also with `--dry-run`: the marker file (present or not examinable), the history entry `producer_differs_seen`, and the latch entry of THIS intent (the same `decided_at`). Under `production` it refuses while any of them stands. An override entry (`producer_concentration_overridden...`, written by a `tick --apply` that passes `--accept-producer-concentration`) waives a memory only when it comes AFTER that memory in the history, by position: an older override waives nothing. With the marker file present and no `producer_differs_seen` entry in the history, no override counts at all (nothing orders it against the detection): run `swap-cli tick --apply` (it writes the detection), pass the flag on that tick, and lock again. The engine writes a new override entry when the flag is passed and the newest latch or detection entry is newer than every override. It only warns under `sepolia` and `rehearsal`. A latch stays on record: if the screen passes again while the intent is still pending, `live.js lock` still refuses; cancel the intent (`swap-cli intent FILE --cancel`) and let a tick record a new one (the flag does not help here: the engine writes an override entry only while the screen still refuses, so on a pass it writes none). And `lock.sh` (a send) asks the engine itself at the moment of sending: it runs a fresh read-only `tick` and sends only if the tick still holds the `lock_eth` intent and the engine's own decision is still the lock, with the screen and every other gate (the note `held: fresh decision was lock_eth`; any `ALERT:` or a wait that says do NOT send it refuses; a tick that fails or cannot be read refuses). The checks of `live.js` stay as the second barrier for a direct `live.js lock` (an outside read, 2026-11-10; a screen, not a defence). `live.js lock` without `--swap` makes no producer check at all and says so.

When no second node is configured: under `production`, with `--single-node-ok` given, the screen fails closed ("producer check needs a second Parano1d node": `--single-node-ok` waives the F5 rule but does **not** relax this check; only `--accept-producer-concentration` does); under `production` without `--single-node-ok` F5 holds the lock first, with its own older reason; under `sepolia` and `rehearsal` the screen says "single node, the producer cross-check is not possible" and warns as everywhere else. When the second node's whole poll failed (`second_error`), F5 holds the lock with its own reason and the screen does not ask that node for headers. The gaps that remain are listed in section 2.

**Compatibility of the second check.** The second-check summary is built afresh on every tick and is not stored in the swap file or in the cache, so older swap files and caches are not affected; `sampled` has a serde default (0) only so that an older JSON output parses again, and a `sampled` of 0 is not accepted by the screen as a cross-check (it reads as "cannot be checked").

**Phase 2: a refusal for a deep reorg (not built).** Refuse when any reorg deeper than 3 blocks is recorded in the window. The node's RPC exposes no reorg history, so it needs `swap-cli` to record its own chain of tip hashes (the cache already detects every mismatch between two ticks and its depth is kept in the summary as a log-only field), and it cannot be retroactive: a window with no record must say "no record", not "no reorg". Open, `TASKS.md` T21-7.

## 5. The override

An explicit flag, **`--accept-producer-concentration`** (no value), on `swap-cli tick`. It follows the pattern of `--single-node-ok`:

- It waives the `production` refusals of the screen: R1 and the fail-closed conditions R3, R4, R5 (the header cross-check) and R6 (the remembered producer difference). It does **not** waive the F5 rules: a single node still needs `--single-node-ok` (and under `production` `--single-node-ok` alone is not enough, the producer check needs the second node or this override), and a second node that cannot be read at all still holds the lock with F5's reason. Under `sepolia` and `rehearsal` it changes nothing (the screen only warns there).
- It is **never implied by a profile.** No profile turns it on, and `--single-node-ok` does not imply it.
- In `scripts/live/env.sh` it is a setting in `env.local.sh` (`ACCEPT_PRODUCER_CONCENTRATION=1`, only the value 1 counts), passed to `swap-cli` only when set; every call that builds the node flags prints a warning on stderr, as `single_node_warning` does.
- `swap-cli` prints the OVERRIDDEN warning (section 6) **on every tick** while the flag is set and the screen would have refused.
- It is **recorded in the swap file's history** once (a log-only entry, `producer_concentration_overridden: ...` with the shares, or the reason the check could not be completed, never an address, no phase change) on the first tick that offers `lock_eth` under the override, when ticking with `--apply`.
- The warning adds that amounts must stay trial-sized.

## 6. Where it plugs in

**Observation.** `NoidObservation` (`swap-cli/src/watch/mod.rs`) gets the optional `producers` summary and the flag `accept_producer_concentration`, both skipped when empty. The reader is `watch::producers::read_producers`; `main.rs` calls it from `tick` after the node poll.

**Decision.** In `bob_action` (`swap-cli/src/engine.rs`), phase `FundingFinal`, after the timing gates have produced a `t_reveal` (so that a Bob who is held back still aborts when the last lock height passes) and after `poseidon_prelock_blocker`: `producer_gate::lock_gate` pushes its notes and, under `production` without the override, returns `Action::Wait` with the ALARM text as the reason. In `received` and `funded` the same finding is said as information when a summary was read.

## 8. What the thresholds buy

The model's chance that the attack works (`docs/formal/t21_results.md`), free ties, at the span the design guarantees (production 361 blocks, sepolia 132), `rho` 2, percent. "Clean" is a start with no private lead; "lead 10" is a start with 10 private blocks.

| true share | production, clean | production, lead 10 | sepolia, clean | sepolia, lead 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 0.30 | 0 | 0 | <0.0001 | <0.0001 |
| 0.35 | 0 | 0 | 0.0022 | 0.0133 |
| 0.40 | <0.0001 | <0.0001 | 0.35 | 1.27 |
| 0.42 | 0.0020 | 0.0053 | 1.60 | 4.76 |
| 0.44 | 0.0675 | 0.15 | 5.49 | 13.5 |
| 0.45 | 0.29 | 0.62 | 9.21 | 20.6 |
| 0.50 | 28.5 | 41.4 | 49.6 | 72.0 |

How to read it. The production limit `X = 35 %` is a measured share. If the producer's share during the swap is no more than 2 sigma (1.9 points) plus the drift allowance (5 points) above the measured one, a measured 35 % stands for a share of at most 42 %: 0.0020 % (clean) and 0.0053 % (lead 10), under 0.1 %. On the same condition the sepolia limit `X = 28 %` stands for at most 35 %: 0.0022 % and 0.0133 %. This reading holds only if the drift judgement holds, and the allowance is not a bound (one-day steps of 5.0, 7.8 and 9.5 points and a 738-block rise of up to 27.8 points were measured, section 4); it does not cover a producer whose blocks are split over several addresses, whose measured share is a lower bound by an unknown amount (section 2). Above the limit the chance climbs fast on the sepolia profile (1.27 % at 0.40, 20.6 % at 0.45) and more slowly on the production profile, whose span is longer. At 0.50 the chance is 28.5 % to 72.0 % depending on the profile and the start; above 0.50 the report's realistic figure is about 100 % (a prepared attacker, `docs/miner-concentration.md`, section 3.4), and only the gate's refusal, when the share is visible, keeps Bob from locking into it. **A larger re-send delay moves the limit down:** with `rho` 10 (about 5 minutes; strict ties) 0.40 gives 0.05 % and 0.45 gives 5.4 % in production (lead 10), so a Bob who re-sends by hand would want a production `X` of about 33 %. That was not adopted (section 9, point 8).

## 9. Decisions taken

These replace the open questions of the design. D1 to D8 are the decisions; the rest of the original list is noted where it was settled by them.

- **D1. The numbers.** Long window 2,880 and short window 720 blocks, both must pass. Single-address limit `X` 35 % for `production`, 28 % for `sepolia` and `rehearsal`. Integer comparison `count * 1000 > limit_permille * N`; a share exactly at the limit passes. The 5-point drift allowance stays a judgement until a daily record (`TASKS.md` T21-1) replaces it with a measurement; it is not a bound on how far a share can move (section 4). The short window is kept.
- **D2. The top-two rule** (the two largest together above 50 % of either window) is a **warning only**, in every profile. It is a printed note, never a refusal.
- **D3. Enforcement by profile.** Under `production` the single-address rule R1 is hard, and so are the fail-closed conditions (headers unreadable or incomplete, a broken parent-hash chain, stale data, a second-node disagreement or a second-node cross-check that could not be read). Under `sepolia` and `rehearsal` the same evaluation runs and everything is a warning; the lock is offered as before. A run in which one person plays both roles on `sepolia` or `rehearsal` therefore needs no flag.
- **D4. The override** is the explicit flag `--accept-producer-concentration` (section 5): it waives the `production` refusals of D3 (the fail-closed ones included), is printed as a warning on every tick, is recorded once in the swap file's history, is never implied by a profile or a default, and does not touch the F5 rules. The helper setting is `ACCEPT_PRODUCER_CONCENTRATION=1` in `env.local.sh`.
- **D5. Phase 2** (a refusal for a deep reorg) is **not built**. The cache-mismatch re-read is built (section 3, step 3), with the depth of the mismatch kept as a log-only field of the summary.
- **D6. The alert for a removed claim** is built (section 10); an automatic re-send is **not** built.
- **D7. Fixtures** use synthetic miner labels only; no real address is in any tracked file.
- **D8. Batching** is built with a fallback to single calls (section 3), and was measured against a local node first.
- **D9. The second node is compared on producers, and a detection is remembered (T21-13).** The second node is asked for the hash and the producer of 32 heights of the short window and 32 of the long window, drawn afresh on each tick (section 4); one `ProducerDiffers` (confirmed by asking the second node for that height once more), or one hash difference at a sampled height below an anchor the second node named again after the sample, writes the marker `<swap file>.producer-differs` and, with `--apply`, the history entry `producer_differs_seen`, which hold Bob's lock gate closed under `production` for the rest of the swap file (section 4, "Remembered detection"); removing the marker reopens nothing, and the ways on are the override or a new swap file. A new rule of Bob's lock gate, decision 2026-11-09; the confirmation, the history memory and the alert for a pending `lock_eth` intent are decision 2026-11-10.

Settled by the above: the original points 2 (top-two: warning only, D2), 3 (short window: kept, D1), 4 (profiles: enforced under production only, D3), 5 (override under production: yes, D4), 6 (phase 2: not built, D5), 7 (re-send: an alert, not an automatic re-send, D6). Still open: point 8 (a lower `X` for a Bob who re-sends by hand: not adopted; `X` stays 35 %), point 9 (recomputing the header hash: not built; the second node is the only check on the headers) and point 10 (order of work: the daily snapshot, `TASKS.md` T21-1, is still not written).

## 10. Bob's re-send after a reorg

**What is true (checked in the code).**

- **The node does not put the transactions of a reverted block back into its mempool.** `readmit_after_reorg` in `vendor/parano1d/noid_mempool/src/pool.rs` only logs ("wallets should resubmit if needed") and removes duplicates that were submitted in the meantime. Its comment says the authorization bytes are not persisted, so a wallet must resubmit with a fresh authorization.
- **`swap-cli` notices and goes back.** When Bob's claim is no longer confirmed, the engine's `ClaimIncluded` branch gets `Included::Reorged`, records the event and sets the file back to `secret_seen` (or to `claim_submitted` when the node shows the claim pending again); the next tick offers `claim_noid` again with a new proof. The test is `f6_a_claim_reorged_out_and_gone_goes_back_to_secret_seen_and_is_proved_again` in `swap-cli/tests/scenarios.rs`.
- **The sending is not automatic.** Offering is not sending. The spend is a step the operator runs by hand (`claim.sh --submit`; without `--submit` it is a dry run). `tick --apply` writes state and sends nothing. No unattended Bob exists.
- So **the re-send delay is the loop interval plus the operator's reaction**: the helpers' loop runs every 30 s by default, and a person needs minutes, i.e. many blocks. It is longer inside the rotation guard (the last 4 blocks before each 144-block epoch boundary, where the claim waits) and while a close of someone else is pending (threat T2).

**Why the delay matters** (`docs/miner-concentration.md`, section 3.5; production, 361 blocks, clean start, strict ties): at a share of 0.45 a re-send after 2 block events gives 0.03 %, after 4 events 0.20 %, after 6 events 0.78 %, **after 10 events 3.6 %, after 20 events 15 %**; at 0.40 it is 0.027 % after 10 events and 1.2 % after 20. A person who re-sends by hand needs 10 to 20 events. The base case of the model, 2 events, is what a loop without an operator step would give.

**Built: the alert.** When a reorg removes Bob's mined claim and the node does not show it pending (the file goes back to `secret_seen`), the tick adds one note that starts with `ALERT:`:

```
ALERT: a reorg removed Bob's NOID claim from the chain and it is not pending. Send it again: `bash $L/bin/claim.sh` is the dry run, check it, then `bash $L/bin/claim.sh --submit` ($L is the helpers' run folder). Then check on the next tick that the claim is in the pool or included, and send it again if it is gone (the node can drop a claim sent just before it handles a reorg); a claim built near the end of a 144-block epoch can expire unconfirmed and must be built again
```

It appears on that transition and nowhere else (a claim that is pending again needs no new send and draws no alert; the next tick draws none). It names no address, amount or transaction. `scripts/live/tick.sh` prints each such note on a line of its own. If `ALERT_URL` is set in `$L/env.local.sh`, `scripts/live/watch.sh` sends a short, fixed message for every `ALERT:` line (`noid-swap run N: claim removed by a reorg; run claim.sh dry run`) with `curl -s -f -m 20 -d MESSAGE -K -`; the URL goes to curl on its standard input, so it is not in the process list. The engine prints the alert on one tick only, so there is no latch by line (a second removal of the claim later in the run alerts again, and the same message twice in one call is sent once). A send that fails is kept in `$L/alert-pending` and tried again at every later tick, up to 20 times, because the line is not printed again; a failure never stops the loop, and the URL is never printed (`scripts/live/README.md`). The alert shortens the delay to the operator's reaction; it does not make it seconds.

**Not built: an automatic re-send.** An opt-in unattended re-send of a claim that was already sent remains a proposal. The risk of automating this one step is small: the claim can only pay Bob (the recipient is committed in the contract), and the secret is public by then. It is not zero: a wrong send burns a fee (at most `max_fee`) and a pending call that is not the right one sits in the first-seen order until it is evicted. Conditions it would need:

1. Opt-in and visible. Off by default; a setting in `env.local.sh` that becomes a flag; printed on every tick while on; never implied by a profile.
2. Only for a claim this file sent. The history shows `claim_submitted` with a recorded txid, then the reorg event, and the chain shows that txid neither confirmed nor pending.
3. The same recipient check: the output owner equals the recipient pinned in the file, the opening equals the file's, the fee is within `max_fee`, and every gate that `claim_noid` has today holds (height below `D`, the target `D - 19`, the rotation guard, no pending call on the instance).
4. A dry-run self-check first: build and prove, check the intent against the recorded values exactly as the dry run does, and print them; any difference sends nothing and raises an alarm.
5. A latch against sending twice: a history entry `auto_resend:<removed txid>` written before the send and checked first, and a fixed maximum of automatic re-sends per swap.
6. A printed record: the removed txid, the new txid, the call height, the tip, the recipient.
7. Submitted to every configured Parano1d node, as `claim.sh --submit` does with a second node.

Whether a stored, already-built claim stays valid across a reorg inside its epoch was not checked (`docs/miner-concentration.md`, section 4.2). It changes the project's rule that every spend is started by the operator, so it needs an explicit decision and a live trial of the alert first (`TASKS.md` T21-8).

## 11. Evaluated after an outside read (2026-11-08; items 3 and 4 are built, the rest is not)

An outside static read of the published version raised the points below. What is real, and what was decided, in order.

1. **Top two above 50 % as a refusal: kept as a warning.** A deliberate majority can split its blocks over two addresses (two shares of 26.5 % for a 53 % miner) and pass the single-address rule; the top-two warning does fire for that split (53 % is above 50 %), but only as a warning. Making it a refusal would catch the two-address split and nothing more: the same party splits over three addresses (17.7 % each) and passes again, while an honest chain such as the one before the dominant address entered (largest 30 %, top two 52 % to 54 % on three of four full days, 10 to 14 producers) would be refused every day with no single party anywhere near a majority. The refusal adds almost nothing against an adversary who knows the rule and costs availability against the honest case. The screen is for a visibly large producer, not for a hidden one (section 2).
2. **A minimum number of distinct producers: not worth building.** With the single-address limit at 35 %, a window with two or fewer producers is already refused (one of them has at least half); three producers pass whenever every share is at most 35 % (each of them then lies between 30 % and 35 %). The case it would add is a window of four to nine producers with every share below 35 %, which is an ordinary chain (10 to 17 producers a day over the 26 days measured, 10 today). It does not stop the split over many addresses either, because the count of distinct producers does not fall when one party splits. If wanted, a floor of 5 would be cheap (one integer compare in `evaluate`); it is not recommended.
3. **Two independent Parano1d nodes in production: built (decision of the owner, 2026-11-08).** The production and sepolia profiles already required a second node for `lock_eth` (`requires_second_node`), and the screen cross-checks one block hash against it; (since 2026-11-09 it also compares the producers of a sample of blocks, item 4) the way around was `--single-node-ok`, which waived the second-node rule and with it the cross-check, so one node's headers alone fed the screen. Now, under `production`, when `--single-node-ok` is given and no second node is, the screen fails closed with `ALARM: producer check needs a second Parano1d node, not locking (T21): ...`; `--single-node-ok` does not relax it, and `--accept-producer-concentration` (the printed override, history entry included) still waives it. Without `--single-node-ok`, F5 speaks first as before. `sepolia` and `rehearsal` stay warning-only and unchanged. A production swap therefore always needs two nodes for the lock (as run 12 did through a tunnel) unless the operator accepts the risk with the override. Independence is not verified by the tool (two URLs can be one machine; the same-host note exists for that).
4. **The second node is asked for a block hash, not for producers: built (2026-11-09; `TASKS.md` T21-13), with two fixes after its first review.** A node can report a true hash beside a false `miner` (section 2). The second node is now also asked for the hash and the `miner` of 32 distinct heights of the short window and 32 of the long window, drawn afresh on each tick (section 4, "Two nodes"), and each is compared with the first node's entry. Fix 1, the sample is stratified: the first version drew only from the long window, but R1 also refuses on the short one (the rising producer), where hiding a 52 % producer needs only about 122 relabelled blocks, 4 % of the long range, so a tick missed about 26 % of the time. Fix 2, a detection is remembered (and Fix 3 widens what is remembered, below): equal hashes with different producers cannot come from two honest nodes, so the first `ProducerDiffers` writes `<swap file>.producer-differs` and Bob's lock gate stays closed under `production` for the rest of the swap (section 4, "Remembered detection"; the tool never removes the file, and since 2026-11-10 a detection cannot be cleared for that swap file at all: the history of the swap file keeps it); before that a tick that caught the lie did not stop the next tick, drawn afresh, from missing it. Fix 3: only `ProducerDiffers` was remembered, but a first node can give each relabelled block a made-up hash too (with a matching false `prev_hash` on the block above it; the anchor stays true), which showed as an ordinary hash `Differs`, refused once and not remembered, so the chance compounded over the ticks again. A different hash at a sampled height below the anchor is now remembered as well, when the second node names the same anchor block again after the sample (two honest nodes that agree on a block agree on every block below it, and an honest reorg would have moved the anchor); a difference at the anchor, a missing height and a timeout are never remembered. The chance of missing is given per swap as the miss chance of the one tick that decides the lock (section 4): at most `(1 - (p - L))^32` for a producer over the limit `L` in one window, and about `(1 - (p - L))^64` when it has the same share in both, which at the production limit of 35 % is 19 %, 3.4 % and 0.26 % for a 40 %, 45 % and 52 % producer in one window (3.7 %, 0.12 % and 0.0007 % in both); the up to five blocks above the anchor are not covered. `SecondCheck` gains `ProducerDiffers { height }` (no address) and `Agrees` gains `sampled`; the screen treats a producer difference exactly like a hash difference. It does not cover several addresses of one party, two lying nodes or one shared lying upstream, a wrong second node or a proxy in front of it (that gives a refusal, never an allowed lock), a node that relabels only a few blocks, the blocks above the anchor, or hashes that are false at blocks that are not sampled (not recomputed). It stays a screen, not a defence. Section 4 lists the timing of a slow second node. Recorded as: a new rule of Bob's lock gate, decision 2026-11-09.
5. **A rise rule: evaluated on the data, not built.** The screen measures a level. The data (`docs/miner-concentration.md`, section 5, drift on sliding windows) shows the failure it cannot see is a rise: a producer that joined after the measurement. A rule that refuses when the short window is well above the long one (for example more than 10 points) would react to a rise a few hours earlier than the level rule, at the price of refusing on noise (the short window's own error is 3.7 points at 2 sigma). In the one observed episode the short window reached 35 % about 11 hours before the long window did (at that moment the long window stood at 29.6 %), and the level rule, which needs both windows to pass, refuses from the short crossing on; a rise rule would have refused some hours before that, and the passing states that were followed by a stretch above 42 % lie before it. A modest gain from a rule that is tuned on one event. Not recommended before more entries are seen.
6. **Three or more addresses give no top-two warning: a stated limit.** A party that splits over three or more addresses is invisible to the screen (section 2, "One miner on many addresses"). A top-three warning would be on every day today (the largest three hold 85.9 %) and carries no information.
7. **The 5-point drift allowance and the 2-sigma margin: rechecked.** For `N` = 2,880, 2 sigma is 1.86 points at the worst case of a share of 0.5, 1.84 near 0.42 and 1.78 near 0.35; the 1.9 in the table is the rounded worst case and the threshold is 35 % either way. The drift allowance of 5 points is smaller than the one-day steps seen in the data (9.5 and 7.8 points once each) and smaller than the 90th percentile (+6.2) and 99th (+16.6) of the growth of a producer's share over the next 738 blocks on sliding windows. The limit is not moved. In the one measured episode a tighter limit did not reduce the share of passing states followed by a stretch above 42 %: their number fell (24, 21, 16, 3 and 0 at 35, 33, 30, 28 and 25 %), but the number of passing states fell with it (231, 210, 87, 18 and 1), so their share was 10 %, 10 %, 18 % and 17 % and reached 0 only where a single state passed; they cluster in the single entry of the dominant address. That is a statement about this episode, not about tighter limits in general, and one episode cannot calibrate a number. The allowance stays a judgement, and it is not a bound: the data shows larger moves.
